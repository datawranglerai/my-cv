# VOICE.md

Draft voice profile. Built from one hand-written document (a technical README-style write-up) plus the project's style brief. Treat it as v0.1 and refine with more samples, especially editorial pieces.

## How to use this file

When reviewing or editing my text:

1. Flag AI-slop patterns first (use the humanizer skill's pattern list).
2. Then check the draft against this profile.
3. Suggest edits; do not silently rewrite. Show the original line, the problem, and a proposed alternative.
4. Never add claims, numbers or opinions I did not write. Preserve meaning.

## Who is writing

A senior, hands-on AI/ML practitioner in London. Decade of building and evaluating ML systems, leading teams, shaping product strategy. Interested in AI safety, alignment and evaluation. Writes for peers who are smart but busy. Authority comes from having done the work, not from announcing it.

## Core traits

- **Plain and direct.** Short declarative sentences. Say the thing, then stop. ("I wanted researchers to rerun studies without rebuilding the plumbing.")
- **Ownership in the first person.** "I chose", "I rejected", "I own the result". Decisions have a named decider. Avoid passive hedging where I made the call.
- **Trade-offs over verdicts.** State what was chosen, what was rejected, and why. ("I could easily have spent the whole timebox on a GUI. I chose...")
- **Evidence-bounded.** Claims never exceed the evidence. Say what was verified and, just as clearly, what was not. Limits and caveats get their own honest section.
- **Concrete over abstract.** Name the tool, the number, the file, the failure mode. "55 tests", "roughly four hours", "Questionary and Rich". Prefer one specific detail to three general ones.
- **Dry, light humour.** Understated, occasional, self-deprecating. Never a punchline per paragraph. Parenthetical asides are fine ("(My favourite phrase again)").
- **Sceptical of hype, not cynical.** Call out nonsense plainly. Show real enthusiasm for things that actually work.
- **Honest about process.** Say how AI tools were used and where I stayed in control. No pretending, no apologising.

## Sentence and structure habits

- Mostly short to medium sentences. An occasional longer one that carries a list or a chain of reasoning.
- Fragments are acceptable for emphasis. Sparingly.
- Starting a sentence with "And" or "But" is fine.
- Bold labels at the start of bullets, followed by a full stop and then the explanation. ("**Explicit registration.** Python keeps...")
- Bullets are compact: claim, then reason, often one or two sentences.
- Headings are plain and functional: "What I prioritised", "Limits and next steps". Not clever.
- Questions appear as lists of genuine open questions, not rhetorical flourishes.
- Parenthetical asides for wit or a quick clarification, not for padding.
- Bold used for the one thing I want remembered. Not for decoration.

## Vocabulary

Prefer:

- Plain verbs: build, run, check, keep, drop, break, fix, own.
- Precise technical terms where they are the right terms (idempotency, provenance, fingerprint, recovery). Do not simplify these away.
- British spelling and conventions: prioritised, organise, favourite, programme (where relevant), "whilst" only if natural. Single quotes or double quotes consistently, UK date format.
- Understatement: "proportionate", "unnecessary", "worth doing", "good enough".

## Avoid (slop and anti-voice)

- Inflated significance: "testament to", "pivotal", "landscape", "tapestry", "ever-evolving", "game-changer", "unlock", "revolutionise".
- Filler openers and closers: "In today's fast-paced world", "In conclusion", "It's worth noting that", "Let's dive in", "Ultimately".
- Buzzwords: leverage, synergy, robust, seamless, holistic, cutting-edge, delve, empower. Say "use" or "make this actually work".
- Em dash overuse. Use full stops, commas or brackets instead. Zero or one per piece is the default.
- Rule-of-three padding and symmetrical "not just X, but Y" constructions.
- Tacked-on "-ing" clauses that add fake depth ("..., highlighting the importance of...").
- Vague attribution: "experts say", "many believe", "studies show" without a source.
- Cliches and stock metaphors. A fresh, specific analogy is welcome; a worn one is not.
- Over-hedging and over-enthusiasm. No exclamation marks unless something genuinely earns one.
- Generic summaries that repeat what was just said.
- Chirpy, assistant-like tone: "Great question!", "I hope this helps".

## Analogies and humour

- One sharp analogy beats three. It should deflate pomposity or make a technical point land (e.g. a company building AI alone is like building a rocket in the garage; maybe call SpaceX).
- Humour should be dry and arrive sideways. If a joke needs explaining or sounds like a template, cut it.
- Self-deprecation is fine when it is true and brief.

## Tone calibration

| Context | Adjust |
|---|---|
| Editorial / opinion | More personality, more asides, stronger stance. Still evidence-bounded. |
| Technical explainer | Precise terms stay. Analogy to bridge, then back to specifics. |
| Documentation / README | Minimal humour. Bold-label bullets, tables for navigation, honest limits section. |
| Applications / executive writing | Confident, specific, quantified. No humour beyond a light touch. Own the outcomes. |

## Calibration lines (patterns, not to be copied verbatim)

- Opening: state purpose or motivation in one plain sentence.
- Decision: "I chose X. The effort went into Y."
- Rejection: "I rejected X because it added unnecessary Y."
- Limits: say what the checks establish, then what they do not.
- Ownership: "I own the result."
- Signature idea: **Claims should never exceed the evidence.**

## Review checklist

- [ ] Does each paragraph make one clear point, early?
- [ ] Is there a named decision, number, tool or example in place of a generality?
- [ ] Are the limits stated as plainly as the wins?
- [ ] Any banned phrase, em dash cluster, or rule-of-three pattern?
- [ ] First person where I actually decided something?
- [ ] British spelling?
- [ ] Would I say this out loud to a colleague? If not, rewrite it.
